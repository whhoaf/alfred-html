Where I Stand on Open Weights as Models Get More Capable

My position is not "open weights are safe." It is that the right question is marginal risk, and that it must be re-measured on every new frontier open model, not settled once.

Author: Alfred Ho  ·  Date: 2026-07-11  ·  Verdict: Open by default, provisionally, with engagement as the real lever

1 Position

I sit close to Kapoor and Bommasani, but for a reason they do not fully spell out. I trust their six-step marginal-risk framework as a method. I do not trust any single number it produces, including Greenblatt's 100,000 fatalities per year, that figure is a stacked-assumption thought experiment from a blog post, not measured evidence.

The honest state of the field is that marginal risk is unproven in both directions.

Underneath sits a prior: open models are, on balance, good. They pull power away from three or four frontier labs, let outside researchers study safety directly, and let the rest of the world, including China, build without permission. Reading the US-China dynamic from inside both cultures, I will not treat the whole open ecosystem as a threat to contain.

2 Uncertainty

My weakest point: I do not have a fixed line. I believe open is good "until the capability threshold," but I cannot yet name that threshold. Greenblatt's candidates, meaningful AI R&D acceleration, autonomous replication, frontier bioweapon uplift, feel directionally right but I have not defended any one.

Two things would move me:

→ A fresh rerun of the framework on a genuinely frontier open model (DeepSeek V4, a Fable 5-class release), not 2023-25 models. At this speed the analysis ages in months.
→ A real incident.

Strongest counter, Greenblatt's own: release is irreversible, so if I misjudge the threshold there is no recall. I accept that asymmetry. It is why my position is provisional, not settled.

3 Coherence with my strategic vision

My vision has always been cooperative development, not a race won by containment, with genuine US-China engagement as its precondition. My open-weight position follows from that, not the other way around. Restriction-first policy assumes the other side can be walled off. The evidence says it cannot.

China will keep shipping open regardless of what the US decides. Given that, the leverage is not a ban. It is engagement with concrete deliverables: shared evaluation standards, incident reporting, and pressure on the jailbreak-resistance gap. That is where a bridge is worth more than a wall, and it is the through-line of everything I have written in this course.
