BlueDot FAIGC · Unit 4 · Exercise: Governance Under Acceleration

What the CERN-Style Global AI Project Needs to Become to Survive the Intelligence Explosion — and What That Costs

Stress test of Vision 8 (Nine Strategic Visions), Alfred's most-compelling pick from Unit 3, against race dynamics and power concentration.

01 · The ProposalThe CERN model

A single multilateral body develops frontier AI on behalf of member states, who hold audit rights and share "launch keys" for high-risk training runs. The pitch: end the race by making the race pointless, everyone owns the outcome together instead of competing for it. Called this the world's best-case direction: cooperative, not zero-sum.

02 · First Points of FailureWhat breaks under acceleration

Ranked #1

Internal Capture

The body itself becomes the exclusive-access prize. A single CEO or director inside the project gains the ability to insert singular or secret loyalty into its AI workforce, no external coup needed, the coup happens from inside the institution built to prevent it.

AI-Enabled Coups, §3.2–3.3

Ranked #1 (tie)

Sabotage / MAIM

Any single project chasing an AI monopoly, however multilateral in name, reads to excluded states as a destabilizing bid for dominance. Rivals maim it: espionage, cyberattack, worst case kinetic. The 180 non-member nations have no key and no exit.

Superintelligence Strategy, §4.1; own Unit 3 answer

Accepted, not primary

Institutional Speed

Treaty-based multilateral governance is slow to build and slow to adapt. Under acceleration, decision windows compress from days to minutes. Real risk, but judged secondary: severity tends to force speed to catch up, as it did for the Manhattan Project.

Preparing for the Intelligence Explosion, §2; four fake graphs (Barak)

03 · The FixMultiple CERNs, not one

Replace the single centralized project with several parallel CERN-style bodies, each fully multilateral, none aligned to a single bloc. No US-led lab, no China-led lab, no EU-led lab standing in for their region, that just re-imports the race this whole model exists to end. Every major power holds a seat and a key in every body.

Move 1 — No single point of failure

If one body is captured, sabotaged, or simply fails, the others continue. Capability, and risk, is no longer pooled in one facility that ends the game if it goes wrong.

Move 2 — Mutual monitoring between bodies

Multiple projects at similar capability levels can audit each other's systems for secret loyalties in a way no project can reliably audit itself. This is the direct fix for the internal-capture failure mode.

AI-Enabled Coups, §5.3: "multiple projects with similar capability levels could deploy AI systems that monitor each other in high-stakes settings, limiting the potential harm from one project's secretly loyal systems."

Move 3 — Multi-key governance inside each body

No single director or CEO holds unilateral unlock power over any one CERN. Checks-and-balances baked into each body's internal structure, plus oversight from multiple governmental bodies (legislatures, courts, allied nations), plus limited centralization (public-private partnership over outright nationalization).

04 · Ngo's LensDoes the fix re-centralize?

Each individual CERN is still a centralized agent internally, unified command, efficient, coherent. But the system of three is closer to a distributed structure: no single node's failure or capture ends the whole arrangement, and the nodes gain the coordination capacity centralized theory says distributed agents usually lack, because they are designed from the start to monitor each other rather than compete.

"The centralization/decentralization tension... is not a matter of policy preferences but rather a structural thing... Centralized agents are more efficient but fragile and corruptible. Distributed agents are more robust but can't coordinate against catastrophic risks." Richard Ngo, Distributed vs Centralized Agents

This redesign is an attempt at what Ngo calls coalitional agency, efficiency and robustness at once. He is explicit this is unsolved in theory. The brief inherits that same open problem rather than resolving it.

05 · What This CostsNamed, not assumed away

The safety system is assumed replicable, and it isn't proven to be

Splitting into three CERNs only works if the alignment and audit tooling that keeps one body coup-proof can be reliably copied into the others. Both AI-Enabled Coups and Nielsen's ASI piece say this is the open problem, not a solved one: auditing a system smarter than its auditor is unreliable in principle, not just in practice. Accepted here as an ongoing, unsolved risk rather than something the redesign fixes. Multiple parties checking each other is judged better than one party checking itself, even without a proof that the checking works.

Consensus is slower than a single crash program

Three multilateral bodies coordinating and auditing each other move slower than one centralized authority under wartime urgency, the actual mechanism that made the Manhattan Project fast was top-down command, the opposite of the consensus this redesign relies on. Bet: severity of stakes forces the pace to close that gap when it matters, the same way nuclear-era institutions accelerated under pressure. Not guaranteed, but judged an acceptable wager against the alternative.

Three sites instead of one, not the alternative of zero

More facilities technically means more places a "recipe for ruin" could leak from. But the comparison is not 3 vs. 0, it is 3 multilateral, mutually-audited bodies vs. today's multipolar field of national and corporate labs racing with no mutual audit at all. On that comparison, three coordinated CERNs is a reduction in leak surface, not an increase.

Bottom Line

The CERN model survives acceleration only if it stops being singular. One global project recreates the exact single-point-of-failure and exclusive-access risks it was built to remove. Three or more fully multilateral bodies, cross-auditing each other with multi-key internal governance, trade some speed and some proliferation surface for something the single-project version could never have: nobody, inside or outside, ever holds all the keys.
